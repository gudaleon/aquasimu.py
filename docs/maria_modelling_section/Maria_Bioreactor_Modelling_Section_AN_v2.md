# Modelling section: mass-balance tool for eggshell–woodchip bioreactors at wetland outflows

*Draft for Maria Dittrich, prepared by Alex Neumann, 22 November 2026 (America/Toronto). Version AN_v2, for the Canada Water Agency Great Lakes Freshwater Ecosystem Initiative proposal (restoring and protecting coastal areas). AQUASIM is the process-model framework and DREAM the Bayesian sampler. Version AN_v1 is unchanged. Placeholders are marked [to confirm with Maria].*

**Purpose.** This component will estimate how much nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) modular bioreactors can trap, and later recycle, at coastal-wetland outflows in the Severn Sound / Georgian Bay area [exact sites to confirm with Maria], with Georgian Bay Forever and Severn Sound. It supports eligible call activities: numerical modelling with bio-physical data collection; pre- and post-implementation assessment, monitoring, and reporting; and transferable science on wetland and nearshore water quality, including nutrients. The reactors use calcined eggshell and woodchips, building on pilots in the Bay of Quinte and the Lake Erie catchment near Windsor. A batch result of up to 80% phosphate removal with 3–5 total weight% eggshell may inform priors and design, but will not be treated as field performance.

**Mass-balance framework.** The analysis opens with an explicit water, N, and P mass balance at each bioreactor and at the wetland outflow it treats. Untreated bypass during high flow is part of the balance. A calibration will not be accepted until these balances close to a reported residual.

- **Water:** inflow = treated outflow + untreated bypass + change in storage + residual.
- **Nitrogen:** inflow load = outflow load + bypass load + denitrification to N₂/N₂O + plant or biomass uptake + immobilization in the woodchip matrix + change in storage + residual.
- **Phosphorus:** inflow load = outflow load + bypass load + sorption and precipitation on calcined eggshell (including hydroxyapatite formation) + plant or biomass uptake + change in storage + residual.

Each residual is a **closure check** and will be reported, not absorbed into a removal term. Intermediate checks and the full input tables will be published alongside the outputs. The model will be versioned, inputs archived, and results recomputed when data are updated. Comparisons with field monitoring are consistency checks on model components, not “validation,” unless the spatial and temporal scale of the observations matches the scale of the prediction.

**Process model: AQUASIM.** The process model will be implemented in AQUASIM, Eawag’s program for identifying and simulating aquatic systems (Reichert 1994, 1998). Each bioreactor will be a mixed-reactor or advective-diffusive (longitudinal flow-through) compartment, with an optional biofilm compartment if depth gradients in the media matter. High-flow bypass will be a linked untreated pathway, so it remains in the mass balance. N and P removal will be user-defined first-order or Michaelis–Menten kinetics with temperature dependence, informed by the woodchip and eggshell literature cited below, but calibrated rather than fixed. AQUASIM’s sensitivity analysis will show which rate constants, residence-time terms, and temperature coefficients are constrained by the monitoring data. Its built-in parameter estimation is not Bayesian and will not be the source of the reported uncertainty.

**Bayesian calibration: DREAM.** Uncertainty will be treated with DREAM (DiffeRential Evolution Adaptive Metropolis), including the DREAM(ZS) variant (Vrugt et al. 2009; Vrugt 2016). DREAM is the outer MCMC sampler: it proposes parameters, a process-model run returns simulated concentrations and loads, and the posterior is updated from the site data and from priors based on the literature and the Quinte and Windsor work. Because AQUASIM’s own fitting is not Bayesian, the two will be coupled either by re-implementing the AQUASIM mass-balance and kinetic equations in R or Python and sampling them with DREAM, or by running AQUASIM in batch from R or Python, with DREAM passing parameters and reading the outputs. Mass-balance closure is checked before a sample is accepted. The main results will be posterior parameter distributions and posterior predictive kilograms of N and P retained per year, with credible intervals, at the reactor and the wetland outflow. Where the data support it, the same relationships will be scaled to the contributing subwatershed and the Severn Sound / Georgian Bay coastal context [extent of scaling to confirm with Maria]. This is methodologically complementary to the laboratory’s proposed Bayesian SPARROW work for the Lake Ontario basin (same philosophy; different basins; not coupled here).

**Deliverables.** (i) Kilograms of N and P retained per year at each reactor and wetland outflow, with credible intervals and a reported closure residual. (ii) Cost per kilogram of N and of P removed, using project cost figures [to be provided by Maria]. (iii) Recycled-nutrient potential of spent eggshell media, with the same uncertainty treatment. (iv) A transferable, versioned modelling tool (AQUASIM process model plus DREAM calibration archive) and an archive of inputs, intermediate checks, and outputs.

**Monitoring needed to close the balances.** Flow at the reactor inlet, treated outlet, and any bypass; water temperature (and dissolved oxygen if feasible); nitrate (NO₃⁻), ammonium (NH₄⁺), total phosphorus (TP), and soluble reactive phosphorus (SRP), sampled routinely and during high-flow events [frequency to confirm with Maria]. Organic N and N₂O may be added if needed to constrain those terms. Spent-media sampling at the end of operating seasons will estimate P accumulated on eggshell and remaining woodchip N-removal capacity.

**Indicative timeline** [duration, start date, and number of sites to confirm with Maria; the call allows up to four years]. Year 1: compile existing pilot and batch data; implement the AQUASIM process model and the DREAM coupling; agree sites and a monitoring design with partners; begin baseline outflow measurements. Year 2: support installation; intensive paired inflow–outflow–bypass monitoring through the first operating season; first DREAM calibration and closure report. Year 3 (and Year 4 if funded): additional seasons, including high-flow events; updated posteriors; subwatershed and coastal scaling; cost-effectiveness and recycled-nutrient estimates; release of the transferable tool and living archive.
