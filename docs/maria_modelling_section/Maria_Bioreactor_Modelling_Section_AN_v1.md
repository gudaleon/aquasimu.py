# Modelling section: mass-balance tool for eggshell–woodchip bioreactors at wetland outflows

*Draft for Maria Dittrich, prepared by Alex Neumann, 21 November 2026 (America/Toronto). Version AN_v1, for insertion into the Canada Water Agency Great Lakes Freshwater Ecosystem Initiative proposal (restoring and protecting coastal areas). Newly written; not an extract from another application. Placeholders are marked [to confirm with Maria].*

**Purpose.** This component will estimate how much nitrogen (N) and phosphorus (P) modular bioreactors can trap, and later recycle, at coastal-wetland outflows in the Severn Sound / Georgian Bay area [exact sites to confirm with Maria], with Georgian Bay Forever and Severn Sound. It supports eligible activities under the call: numerical modelling with associated bio-physical data collection; pre- and post-implementation assessment, monitoring, and reporting; and transferable science on wetland and nearshore water quality, including nutrients. The reactors use calcined eggshell and woodchips, building on pilots in the Bay of Quinte and the Lake Erie catchment near Windsor. The team’s batch finding that up to 80% of phosphate could be removed with 3–5 total weight% eggshell may inform priors and design, but laboratory and pilot results will not be treated as field performance.

**Mass-balance framework.** The analysis opens with an explicit water, N, and P mass balance at each bioreactor and at the wetland outflow it treats. Untreated bypass during high flow is part of the balance.

- **Water:** inflow = treated outflow + untreated bypass + change in storage + residual.
- **Nitrogen:** inflow load = outflow load + bypass load + denitrification to N₂/N₂O + plant or biomass uptake + immobilization in the woodchip matrix + change in storage + residual.
- **Phosphorus:** inflow load = outflow load + bypass load + sorption and precipitation on calcined eggshell (including hydroxyapatite formation) + plant or biomass uptake + change in storage + residual.

Each residual is a **closure check** and will be reported, not absorbed into a removal term. All intermediate mass-balance checks and the full input tables will be published alongside the outputs. The model will be versioned, inputs archived, and results recomputed when data are updated. Comparisons with field monitoring are consistency checks on model components, not “validation,” unless the spatial and temporal scale of the observations matches the scale of the prediction.

**Process representation and uncertainty.** Woodchip media supply organic carbon and support heterotrophic denitrification under low-oxygen conditions (Schipper et al. 2010). Nitrate removal depends strongly on hydraulic residence time and temperature: beds with residence times shorter than about six hours remove significantly less nitrate, and rates increase with temperature (Addy et al. 2016 report a Q₁₀ of 2.15). Calcination of eggshell converts CaCO₃ toward CaO and Ca(OH)₂, which react with dissolved phosphate to form hydroxyapatite; spent, P-loaded media has been examined as a potential fertilizer amendment (Lee et al. 2022). Removal will be represented with first-order or Michaelis–Menten kinetics whose rate constants, residence-time dependence, and temperature sensitivity are calibrated, not fixed at literature values.

Uncertainty will be treated in a Bayesian framework, consistent with the Arhonditsis laboratory’s approach. Priors from the literature and from the Quinte and Windsor work will be updated with site monitoring to yield posterior predictive distributions of annual N and P load reductions, with credible intervals, at the reactor and the wetland outflow. Where the data support it, the same relationships will be used to scale estimates to the contributing subwatershed and the Severn Sound / Georgian Bay coastal context [extent of scaling to confirm with Maria]. This is methodologically complementary to the laboratory’s proposed Bayesian SPARROW work for the Lake Ontario basin (same load-attribution philosophy; different basins; not coupled here).

**Deliverables, monitoring, and timeline.** **Deliverables.** (i) Kilograms of N and P retained per year at each reactor and wetland outflow, with credible intervals and a reported closure residual. (ii) Cost per kilogram of N and of P removed, using project cost figures [to be provided by Maria]. (iii) Recycled-nutrient potential of spent eggshell media, with the same uncertainty treatment. (iv) A transferable, versioned modelling tool and an archive of inputs, intermediate checks, and outputs.

**Monitoring needed to close the balances.** Flow at the reactor inlet, treated outlet, and any bypass; water temperature (and dissolved oxygen if feasible); nitrate (NO₃⁻), ammonium (NH₄⁺), total phosphorus (TP), and soluble reactive phosphorus (SRP), sampled routinely and during high-flow events [frequency to confirm with Maria]. Organic N and N₂O may be added if needed to constrain those terms. Spent-media sampling at the end of operating seasons will estimate P accumulated on eggshell and remaining woodchip N-removal capacity.

**Indicative timeline** [duration, start date, and number of sites to confirm with Maria; the call allows up to four years]. Year 1: compile existing pilot and batch data; build the versioned mass-balance model; agree sites and a monitoring design with partners; begin baseline outflow measurements. Year 2: support installation; intensive paired inflow–outflow–bypass monitoring through the first operating season; first Bayesian calibration and closure report. Year 3 (and Year 4 if funded): additional seasons, including high-flow events; updated posteriors; subwatershed and coastal scaling; cost-effectiveness and recycled-nutrient estimates; release of the transferable tool and living archive.
